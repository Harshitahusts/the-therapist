# Behavioural activation

## The cycle of low mood and withdrawal
When mood is low, people naturally do less: they cancel plans, stop hobbies and stay in. This makes sense in the moment but often deepens low mood, because there are fewer chances for enjoyment, achievement and connection. Behavioural activation is a well-researched approach that works by gently reversing this cycle.

## Action before motivation
A key idea is that motivation often follows action rather than coming first. Waiting to "feel like it" can mean waiting a long time. Doing a small thing, even without much enthusiasm, can lift mood a little and make the next step easier.

## How to try it
1. Notice what you've been doing and how it affects your mood. A quick daily note can help.
2. Pick activities that bring a sense of pleasure (something enjoyable), achievement (something that gives a sense of getting things done) or connection (time with others), and that fit what matters to you.
3. Make them small and specific: "walk to the end of the street after lunch" rather than "exercise more".
4. Schedule them, as you would an appointment.
5. Afterwards, notice how you felt, without judging the result.

## When it's hard to start
Break tasks into the smallest possible first step ("open the laptop", "put on shoes"). Plan for obstacles. Expect some days to go less well; the aim is a pattern over time, not perfection.

## Values
Choosing activities linked to what matters to you, such as relationships, health, learning or creativity, tends to make them more rewarding and sustainable.
