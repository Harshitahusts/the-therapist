# Sleep habits

## Sleep and mood
Sleep and mood affect each other. Poor sleep makes stress and low mood harder to manage, and worry makes sleep harder. Small, consistent changes often help more than big ones.

## Helpful habits
- Keep a regular wake-up time, including weekends. This anchors the body clock.
- Get daylight early in the day.
- Wind down for 30 to 60 minutes before bed with something calm.
- Limit caffeine after early afternoon and avoid heavy alcohol near bedtime; alcohol fragments sleep.
- Keep the bedroom cool, dark and quiet where possible.
- Reduce screens and work in bed, so the bed is linked with sleep.

## If you can't sleep
Lying awake and trying hard to sleep usually backfires. If you've been awake for what feels like 20 minutes or so, get up, do something quiet and dull in dim light, and go back to bed when sleepy. Avoid checking the time repeatedly.

## Racing mind at night
Try a "worry time" earlier in the evening: write down worries and one next step for each, then close the notebook. At night, a reminder that "I've already written that down" can help. A slow breathing exercise or body scan can also settle the mind.

## When to get help
If sleep problems last for several weeks, affect daytime life, or come with loud snoring or pauses in breathing, it's worth speaking to a doctor. CBT for insomnia (CBT-I) is a well-supported treatment.
