# Work stress and impostor feelings

## Impostor feelings
Many capable people feel like a fraud, especially in new roles. The thought "I'll be found out" is common and often strongest when stepping into something unfamiliar. Feeling out of your depth at the start is a normal part of learning, not proof of incompetence.

## Things that help with a new role
- Expect a learning curve; nobody is expected to know everything in the first weeks.
- Write down what you're learning and small wins; it's easy to forget progress.
- Ask questions early. It's usually seen as engagement, not weakness.
- Separate feelings from evidence: "I feel unprepared" versus "What do I actually know about how I'm doing?"
- Identify one or two people you can check in with.

## Managing stress at work
- Break large tasks into smaller next actions.
- Prioritise: what actually needs to happen today?
- Protect breaks, meals and sleep; they keep thinking clear.
- Notice signs of burnout: exhaustion, cynicism, and feeling ineffective. Persistent burnout is worth discussing with a manager, HR, a doctor or a professional.

## Job searching
Long periods of applications without replies are discouraging and very common. It can help to set process goals ("send three tailored applications this week") rather than outcome goals, and to keep some structure and enjoyable activity in the day.
