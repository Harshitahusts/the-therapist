# Ideas from acceptance and commitment therapy (ACT)

## The aim
ACT helps people make room for difficult thoughts and feelings while still moving towards what matters to them. It doesn't try to get rid of painful experiences; it changes our relationship with them.

## Cognitive defusion
Thoughts can feel like facts. Defusion means stepping back and seeing a thought as a thought. Simple ways to practise:
- Add "I'm having the thought that..." in front of it: "I'm having the thought that I'm not good enough."
- Name the story: "Ah, the 'I'm falling behind' story again."
- Notice the thought, then return attention to what you're doing.

## Acceptance
Acceptance means allowing feelings to be present without fighting them, not liking or approving of them. Struggling hard against anxiety often amplifies it. You might notice where you feel it in your body, breathe around it, and let it be there while you carry on.

## Values
Values are chosen directions, like being a caring friend, learning, or contributing. Unlike goals, they are never "finished". Asking "What matters to me here?" or "What kind of person do I want to be in this situation?" can guide choices when feelings are loud.

## Committed action
Small actions in the direction of your values, taken even while uncomfortable feelings are present, are at the heart of ACT. "What is one small step I could take today that moves me towards what matters?"

## Being present
Coming back to the present moment, through the senses or the breath, helps when the mind is caught in worries about the future or regrets about the past.
