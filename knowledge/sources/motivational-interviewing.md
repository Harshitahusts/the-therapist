# Ideas from motivational interviewing

## Ambivalence is normal
Most people feel two ways about change: part of them wants it, part of them doesn't. Motivational interviewing treats this as normal, not as resistance or laziness. Pushing someone to change tends to make them argue for the other side.

## OARS
- Open questions: "What would you like to be different?"
- Affirmations: recognising strengths and effort: "You've kept going through a lot."
- Reflections: saying back what you heard, including both sides of mixed feelings.
- Summaries: drawing together what the person has said.

## Evoking the person's own reasons
People are more persuaded by reasons they say themselves. Useful questions:
- "What makes this important to you?"
- "If things stayed the same, how might that be in a year?"
- "On a scale of 0 to 10, how important is this change to you? Why that number and not a lower one?"
- "What would help you move one point higher?"

## Respecting autonomy
The person decides. Phrases like "It's your call" and "What do you think might work for you?" protect autonomy and usually make change more likely.
