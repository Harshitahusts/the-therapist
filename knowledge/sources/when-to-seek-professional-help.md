# When to seek professional help

## A companion app is not a replacement for care
Talking things through can help, but an AI companion cannot diagnose, treat, or provide therapy. Professional support can make a real difference, and asking for it is a sign of strength.

## Signs it may be time to reach out
- Low mood, anxiety or stress that lasts for more than two weeks or keeps coming back.
- Difficulty managing everyday life: work, studies, relationships, eating or sleeping.
- Losing interest in things you used to enjoy.
- Using alcohol or drugs to cope.
- Panic attacks, intrusive thoughts, or experiences that feel frightening or confusing.
- Thoughts of self-harm or suicide.

## Where to start
- A GP or primary-care doctor is often the first step; they can talk through options and referrals.
- Licensed therapists, counsellors, psychologists or psychiatrists.
- Workplace or university wellbeing services.
- Trusted people: friends, family, mentors, community or faith leaders.

## In a crisis
If you are in immediate danger or might act on thoughts of harming yourself or someone else, contact local emergency services or a crisis line now, or ask someone nearby to help. International directories such as findahelpline.com list services by country.
