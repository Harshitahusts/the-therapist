# Grounding and breathing for anxiety and panic

## What panic is
A panic attack is a sudden surge of intense fear with strong body sensations: racing heart, breathlessness, dizziness, trembling, tightness in the chest, or a feeling of unreality. It is the body's alarm system firing when there is no immediate danger. Panic attacks are very unpleasant but usually peak within about ten minutes and pass.

Important: chest pain, fainting, or symptoms that feel different from usual anxiety should be checked by a medical professional. If in doubt, seek medical help.

## Slow breathing
Breathing slowly, with the out-breath a little longer than the in-breath, signals safety to the body. For example: breathe in gently through the nose for about four counts, and out through the mouth for about six counts. Repeat for a few minutes. Don't force big breaths; slow and gentle works best.

## 5-4-3-2-1 grounding
Bring attention to the senses: name five things you can see, four you can feel, three you can hear, two you can smell, and one you can taste. This anchors attention in the present.

## Other grounding ideas
- Feel your feet on the floor and press them down.
- Hold something cold, or run cool water over your hands.
- Describe your surroundings in detail, out loud or in your head.
- Count backwards from 100 in sevens.

## Riding the wave
Fighting panic often feeds it. Noticing "this is anxiety, it's uncomfortable, and it will pass" and letting the sensations rise and fall, like a wave, can reduce their power over time.

## Afterwards
After intense anxiety, people often feel drained. Gentle self-care, water, and rest help. If panic attacks are frequent or lead to avoiding places, a GP or mental-health professional can help; panic responds well to treatment such as CBT.
